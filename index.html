Operating stance

We do not compete with healthy public-interest technology.

When a strong privacy-preserving or open-source project already serves people well, we respect it, use it, and point people toward it. VoxonLabs builds where there is a real gap and where the work can be maintained responsibly.

Foundation layer

Voxon Shield answers trust questions without revealing identities.

Shield is the proposed reusable API, SDK, policy engine, and safety layer for VoxonLabs applications. An app should ask whether a user is a verified adult human and receive a short-lived signed assertion. It should not receive a passport, name, date of birth, address, selfie, or provider data.

Open architecture RFC Review Shield
Assertions, not identity records

Applications receive derived facts such as verified human, age over 18, and eligible. Raw identity packages stay outside the application boundary.

Pairwise pseudonyms

EverCommons, a forum, and a future dating app should each receive different internal subjects for the same person.

Modular safety

Verification, passkey authentication, uniqueness, anti-spam, messaging controls, and explicit-content policy stay separate and reviewable.

First application

EverCommons is the first proof on top of Shield.

EverCommons Social is the first application concept: an open-source photo/video network for real people, creators, communities, and public-interest projects. It starts small, publishes its rules, asks Shield for only minimal trust assertions, and grows only when capacity, moderation, privacy, and economics are ready.
